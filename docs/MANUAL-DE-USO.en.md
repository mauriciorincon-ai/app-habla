# Hablemos San — User manual (English)

> For the parent who uses the app with their child. Plain language, no jargon. Written in English, not
> translated line by line: the Spanish manual (`MANUAL-DE-USO.md`) covers the same features.

## What this app is

**Hablemos San** answers one question every day: _"what can I do with my child today?"_ Each day it offers one
technique with research behind it, explained in 30 seconds, with **one concrete line you can say today** and an
activity to do together. Some days the activity includes a game where **your child's voice moves a balloon on the
screen**.

It is **home practice that encourages speech**. It is not therapy, it does not assess anyone, and it makes no
promises about when anything will happen. It sits beside, and never in place of, your child's own speech and
language therapy.

**The app guides the adult; it does not replace the adult.** What moves a child's speech along is time with you.
The screen is only the excuse to play together.

## First steps

1. Open the app in the browser of your tablet or phone (Chrome on Android works well).
2. **Install it as an app** (optional, recommended): in the browser menu, choose "Add to home screen". It then
   opens full screen and works offline.
3. The first time, it asks two things: what your child is called at home (optional) and up to three topics they
   like. **All of it stays on the device**; there are no accounts and no sign-up.
4. Want to change the nickname or topics later? Go to **Settings → "Nickname and topics" → "Change nickname and
   topics"**. Changing them erases nothing: progress stays as it is.

## Language: Español or English

The app speaks **Spanish and English**, and each language is written for that language. By default it **follows
your browser's language**. To pick the other one, open **Settings → "Idioma / Language"** and tap **Español** or
**English** (the chosen one shows a «✓»). The change is immediate, needs no reload, and stays on the device like
any other setting.

- **What changes:** Today, the daily capsule (all 50), first use, the games, the Studio, the Goal, the Path,
  Settings, and the brochure page `/conoce`, which has its own switch.
- **The daily capsule is the same** in both languages: switching language never changes what is on for today.
- **Words and Twins, in English (S8):** both games **are practised in English**: the words, the pictures and the
  look-alike sound pairs are English ones (not the Spanish ones put into English), and the cues you record in the
  Studio come in English as well. The voice games (balloon and rocket)
  play the same in both languages.
- **The weekly Goal, in English (S8):** it writes and suggests in English, with its own spelling dictionary, and
  no longer warns you about anything. Each language has its own.
- **`/mirada` and the capsule catalog, in both languages (S8):** "The pyramid, at home" and the catalog carry a
  **Español · English** switch at the top that changes the whole document without reloading; it follows the
  language you chose in the app. When printed, the switch does not appear.
- **Privacy:** switching language sends nothing anywhere.

## How to use it

### Today — your daily answer

On opening you see **the capsule of the day**: the technique, why it works, **your line for today**, and the
activity. When you have done it, the app asks "Did you do today's activity?" and you tap **"Yes, we did it"**.

- Each day brings a different capsule; none repeats until all have been used.
- Today's capsule **does not change** if you close and reopen the app.
- At the bottom it counts how many days you have practised together **in total**. It grows each time you tap "Yes,
  we did it". There are **no streaks and no penalties**: that number never goes down. If you skip a day, nothing is
  lost. What counts is coming back.
- Under "Where does this come from?" you can see the study each capsule rests on.
- **Two domains (S9):** below the speech capsule you will find **"Also today · eye contact"**: just one
  eye-contact activity a day, with **your line first** (you say it), then the activity, how long it takes and what
  not to do. It changes by date, does not repeat within the week and follows the pace you set. **It is
  screen-free:** you do it together, looking at your child. "See them all in The pyramid, at home" opens the full
  document. Underneath there is also a way in to **"A story to read together"**.

### The voice games — "their voice moves the world"

On Today there is a permanent card, **"Voice games"**. Open it and choose among **five games** (the fifth is experimental). None asks the child
for words: they respond to voice (or to taking part), not to vocabulary.

**Four of them listen to the microphone** (balloon, rocket, word and picture, and the vowel map). Before any of those, the same
three things always happen:

1. **Your line first.** The app shows the line you will say. You lead the game; the child does not play alone
   (there is no "child alone" mode, and that is on purpose).
2. **Microphone permission.** The browser asks whether the app may listen. You need to accept in order to play.
3. **Two seconds of quiet ("shhh…").** The app measures how your home sounds, such as the fan or the TV, so it does
   not mistake that noise for your child's voice. If it is very noisy, the app says so openly and you decide
   whether to go on or measure again.

**"Word twins" uses no microphone** (see below): no permission and no quiet measurement.

Tap **"We played"** whenever you like: there is no minimum time, no required goal, and never a game over. If you
tap **"Exit"** inside a game, you return to your line screen (the microphone turns off right there) and **the game
restarts completely**: when you go back in, the counters start from zero and nothing of the earlier session
remains. From your line screen, **"← Games"** takes you back to the five.

#### 1. The balloon — a voice that keeps going

The balloon **moves forward only while your child's voice is sounding** ("aaaaah"). If the voice stops, the
balloon slows down gently and waits: what it already gained is never lost. Every **3 seconds of voice added up**,
the balloon crosses the line and completes **one lap**. The app celebrates it right then ("It did 2 laps!") with
**confetti and streamers** that fall once over the line and go away; the game does not pause. Then the balloon
comes back in from the left to keep flying. **Laps never run out: the game does not end by itself.** Play as long
as you like and, when you decide, tap **"We played"**. Then comes the celebration, which says exactly what was
measured, in **three separate things**:

- _"Their voice sounded 7.3 seconds!"_ — the **total** for the try, adding up every stretch when voice was heard.
  This is what made the balloon fly.
- _"With that voice the balloon did 2 full laps."_ — the **same laps** you celebrated live, said once more at the
  end. (In calm mode there is no lap line, so this sentence does not appear.)
- _"And the longest time they kept going was 2.4 seconds in a row"_ — the longest **unbroken stretch**. It is the
  only thing that allows the app to say they _held_ the sound, and it is the skill that is really being practised.

A breath does not take the stretch away: a short pause to take air does not count as breaking it. **The app never
says your child held a sound longer than they did**, even when adding the stretches up would give a nicer number.

#### 2. The rocket — a voice that goes up and down

The rocket listens to the **pitch** of the voice: when the voice goes up, the rocket goes up; when it comes down,
the rocket comes down. No word has to be said. **A vowel stretched like a siren is exactly what we are after**
("aaaAAAaaa"). Do it yourself first, big and laughing.

Each time the voice **really goes up and comes down**, the rocket earns a milestone: **confetti** bursts and a
counter appears: _"It went up and down 2 times!"_ On the **next rise** of the voice, a **layer of clouds and stars
slides down the screen**. The world rushes by while the rocket climbs, and it feels like going higher and higher,
as if you were looking out of a window. **Milestones never run out: the game does not end by itself.** Play as long
as you like and, when you decide, tap **"We played"**. The celebration gives **the same number as the counter**: the
real number of times your child's voice changed direction. Playing with the voice by stretching it, raising it and
lowering it is a way of exploring sound, and it is just what this stage needs.

One honest detail: the rocket's **height** is always the pitch sounding at that moment, which is why it comes down
when the voice comes down. It is not a punishment; it is simply the pitch. The total climb is counted by the passing
sky and the counter, not by the rocket's position.

#### 3. Word and picture — the name lives in the thing

A **large picture appears with its word written under it** (dog, car, water…). The pictures come from the **topics
you chose** for your child.

Your part is the important one: **say the name of the picture once**, "Dog!", and **wait three seconds in
silence**. The picture lights up with **any sound** your child makes: an attempt, a "puh", a babble. The moment is
visible: the frame gives a short **pop** and a halo spreads out once. **The app does not require the word and does
not judge whether it was said well**: it only knows, and only claims, that their voice sounded. Tap **"Another
picture"** whenever you want to change.

**And if they did say the word? You say so.** At the **bottom of the screen**, below the big buttons, there is a
discreet control in small print, _"Did they say «dog»? You tap it"_, placed there on purpose, out of the child's
sight (it is yours, not theirs). When you tap it, a **flock of balloons** rises over the picture, because saying
the word deserves more than just any sound. (If you turned on "reduce animation", the balloons do not fly but the
celebration still shows: the picture turns green and says so in words.)

That button is **yours, not your child's**, and it is the only way to switch that celebration on: **the app does
not recognise words and will not pretend to.** It is not that we cannot build it. Doing it well would mean sending
your child's voice to a server (never), and doing it badly would mean telling them "no" when they **did** say the
word. Automatic recognition makes mistakes most with children who are just learning to speak. You were there, and
you know. At the end, the celebration says it plainly: _"And **you** heard 3 words. That is something you know,
because you were there."_

#### 4. Word twins — two pictures that sound almost the same

**Two pictures appear at once**, and their words differ by a single sound: _pato_ and _gato_, _mano_ and _mono_,
_foca_ and _boca_. These are called **minimal pairs**, and practising that contrast helps sharpen speech. Your
line: **point at both, ask your child to say ONE**, then **mark the one you heard** on the discreet _"Which did you
hear?"_ line at the bottom. (This game practises Spanish words; see the Language section.)

**How a round goes:**

- **Touching a picture makes it sound in your voice** (if you recorded that word in the Studio; it carries a small
  speaker badge in the corner). Your child can touch them and explore the pair. Touching only plays the sound and
  **never marks anything**.
- When your child says a word, **you mark it** under _"Which did you hear?"_. The chosen picture then
  **celebrates**: it lights up, the word plays in your voice, and _"They said «pato»!"_ appears. The child's party
  is in the pictures; the discreet buttons are yours.
- **"Next pair"** is yours to tap when you want to go on. You can repeat, touch the pictures again, or stay a
  while. At the top, _"Words you heard: N"_ keeps count for the session.

This game is **different from the other three**:

- **It uses no microphone. It records and analyses nothing.** The app does not hear the word: **you are the one who
  hears it**, which is why the marking buttons are yours. It is shared play, pure and simple.
- **There is no right or wrong answer for your child.** Any attempt counts; if today they only want to look, tap
  **"Today we just look at this one"** and carry on without hurry.
- The celebration is honest: it counts **how many rounds you played** and in how many **you** marked something. It
  never says your child "got it right". And the picture's party celebrates **what you marked**, not a verdict from
  the app.
- **It needs your child to already say single words.** If your stage is _"sounds and attempts"_, the game tells you
  so kindly and invites you to come back when it fits. You can change the stage in Settings.

If you recorded your voice (below), each picture has a **speaker button** so your child can hear the word in the
family's voice before trying it.

#### 5. The vowel map — ah, eh, ee, oh, oo (new · S10 · experimental)

A game **still being tested**, and the screen says so ("Experimental · still testing"). Your child **stretches a
vowel** (ahhh, ehhh, eeee, ohhh, oooo) and the **spot on the map** that sounds most like it lights up. Every spot has
its **own letter, a shape with a name** (circle, triangle, diamond, square, star) **and a colour**: colour is never
the only signal.

- **Go first**, slowly, with a clearly shaped mouth. If they would rather just watch today, that is fine too.
- **Always "maybe":** the app measures the **shape of the sound** and says "Maybe it sounds like…", with a margin of
  error (especially with very high voices). **It never says a vowel was right or wrong**: you know, because you hear it.
- **What counts at the end:** how many spots **sounded steadily** and how many times it **moved from one to
  another**. With no voice, the map just waits and invents nothing.
- **What is kept:** only those two numbers, for the path. **Not the voice, not the Hz, not the vowel it thought it heard.**
- It has **calm mode** (no counters) and **"Measure again"** like the others. The sound is analysed on the spot and
  discarded.

### Your family's voice — record your voice for the games

The games can **sound with the voice of mom or dad** instead of staying silent. It is optional, and you manage it
from **Settings → "Your family's voice" → "Record my voice"** (or straight from the **Studio** screen).

**How it works:**

- The Studio shows you, one at a time, the **words and phrases** you can record (the words of the pictures, some
  game invitations, and the honest celebrations). Tap **"Record"**, say the word, **listen to how it came out**, and
  **keep it** or **record it again**. A bar shows how much you have recorded. While you record, **another bar moves
  with your voice**: if it moves when you speak, you are being heard, so you never record blind. (That bar is only
  for looking; it saves nothing.) And when you play back any recording, **a small bar fills as the audio advances**,
  so you know it is playing and how much is left.
- Under **"Your voice is on its way"** each group (Words · Game invitations · Celebrations) has its own **"Record"**
  button. You can go straight to the group you want, for example recording the two invitations without going
  through all the words. The big button ("Record more") remains the usual guided route, which suggests first what
  helps your child most.
- Under **"What you have recorded"**, recordings are grouped by those same three groups, numbered within each one,
  with **Play** (it sounds with its progress bar in the row) and **Delete** buttons. Delete asks for a **second
  tap**: the button turns red and says "Sure?", so a hurried finger does not erase anything by accident. If you
  change your mind, it disarms itself after a few seconds.
- When there are recordings, "word and picture" and "word twins" show a **speaker button** (large, so your child can
  tap it), and the word **plays by itself** when the picture appears. That way the name "lives in the thing", in a
  voice your child knows.
- **In the balloon and the rocket too:** if you recorded the game invitations ("Make your voice sound: aaaaah" and
  "Make a siren voice: aaaAAAaaa"), a speaker appears under the title that says them in your voice. While your
  recorded voice is playing, **the game stands still**: the meter waits for your child's real voice, so the balloon
  never flies because of a recording.
- You can **switch your family's voice off** with the _"Use your family's voice"_ switch **without deleting
  anything**, and switch it on again whenever you like.

**The most important part — what is recorded and what NEVER is:**

- **YOUR voice is recorded**, the adults' voice. The screen says it clearly: _"Your child's voice is never
  recorded."_ The Studio is for the adult.
- **Your child's voice is never recorded**, not even here. The games that listen to the microphone still save
  nothing of their voice (see Privacy).
- **The voice bank lives ONLY on this device.** It is not uploaded to the internet, not copied to any server, and
  not kept in the project's repository. If you change device, the recordings **do not travel**: you record them
  again where they will be used.
- You can delete **only the voice bank** (touching nothing else) with _"Delete only the voice bank"_, or delete
  everything from _"Delete my data"_.

> An honest detail: the app records **fixed phrases**. The final celebration that says a number, such as "Their
> voice sounded 7.3 seconds!", **cannot** be recorded in your voice, because the number changes every time; those
> stay as text. Your voice plays in what always says the same thing (the names, "Their voice came out!", "They said
> the word!").

### Speech stage — how to choose it

Under **Settings → "How does your child talk today?"** you choose the stage. The daily capsules fit what your child
does TODAY, not what we might wish they did:

- **Sounds and attempts.** Still exploring: makes sounds, points, leads you by the hand. Gestures and attempts are
  their way of talking, and you answer them by naming things.
- **Single words** _(the recommended one, and the one the app starts with)_. Says words one at a time: "water",
  "mama", "car". Does not put them together, and there is no need to hurry them. **This is where the app does most
  of its work.**
- **First phrases.** Sometimes joins two words: "more water", "big car". This stage **never switches on by
  itself**: you have to choose it by hand, on purpose.

**Changing stage erases nothing.** Progress for each stage is kept separately and the days you practised are not
lost. If in doubt, stay with _single words_.

### The path — how things are going, with no marks

In the header of Today there is a link, **"The path"**. It is the honest answer to "how are we doing?". It shows,
**week by week**, only what really happened: the days you practised together, the capsules completed, the different
words you practised, how many pictures your child lit up with their voice, the balloon's laps, the times their
voice went up and down, the twins rounds, the words you heard them say, and the seconds of their voice in the
balloon (the total and the longest unbroken stretch). Each line appears only when it has something to tell. There is
also a section of celebratory **milestones**, **16 possible ones**: from "your first time practising together" and
the first time with each game, to running totals such as "25 days of practice in all". Once earned, they are never
lost.

One thing worth knowing: **the path counts what reached the celebration.** End the game with "We played" so the try
is recorded; if you leave with "Exit" halfway through, that stretch is not counted (on purpose: only what the game
has already shown you is recorded).

What the path will **never** do: give your child a mark, a rating, a percentage or a deadline; compare them with
another child; or punish them for a quiet week (a week with few days is simply a small number, with no drama). It is
not an evaluation or a report card. It is the warm record of what you did together. At first it will be empty; play
for a few days and it comes to life.

### The weekly goal — in tune with their therapy

In your child's therapy sessions, whether speech and language, occupational therapy, psychology, or teaching, were
you asked to work on something this week: animals, food, bath time? In the header of Today, **"Goal"** lets you
write it down. The app then **puts that first**: the capsule of the day, the pictures in "word and picture", and
what is next to record in the Studio all line up with your goal. It is optional: with no goal, everything stays as
it always is.

Everything happens on **one card**: the field, the suggestions, the notice about what it finds, and the save button,
which **names exactly what it will save** ("Save «animals»").

While you type, the app **keeps you company; it does not scold**. If you stop halfway through a word ("anim"), it
suggests what you may be thinking of, in **two groups, each with its label**. The **green** chips are terms that
**are in the app** and line up the capsule and the games. The **neutral** chips are correctly spelled words of the active language
that **the app does not have** ("medi" → “media”, “medium”): they change nothing in the games, but they help your
goal be written correctly. They come from a dictionary of the most common words of the language that travels inside
the app (no internet needed, like everything here; there is one in Spanish and one in English, and the app uses the
one for the active language). The field also uses your browser's or keyboard's spell-checker
(the usual red underline). The names of the topics **as you see them in Settings** also work: typing "transport"
lines up everything under that topic.

**In English, the aligner and the dictionary work in English**: write "animals" and the app lines things up.

If you type with a spelling mistake ("animles") and tap save, the app **asks first**: _"Did you mean «animales»?"_
You can fix it with one tap or save it as typed (writing freely stays your right: in therapy they may ask for
something the app does not have). A **correctly spelled** word never gets that question: "media" or "colors" are
saved the first time, even if they line nothing up.

Once the word is complete, the app tells you **honestly** how much it finds, and it counts **only what your child
will actually see**: the capsules for their stage and the pictures from the topics you chose ("with «animales», the
app puts first N capsules · M pictures"). If you type something that is not in the app's content, such as "colors",
which deliberately does not exist, it tells you so openly instead of pretending it lined something up. And if you
type something that does exist but is **outside what your child sees today** (a topic you did not choose during
first use), it also says so clearly: the games do not change, although the Studio will suggest recording those
words first. The goal **does not expire by itself**: it stays until you change or remove it (and removing it leaves
everything as it was). It always works **within the stage** you chose and never skips it.

The card for the **active goal shows its state** with three signals at once: colour, icon, and words (designed for
colour blindness too, so each state has its own **shape**, not only its colour):

- **Green with the target** when your child's games are going to change, with the breakdown of what is lining up
  ("Lining up now: 4 capsules · 6 pictures").
- **Blue with the info circle** when something needs your attention, for example "today's capsule yes, but the
  pictures in the games do not change: that topic is not among the ones you chose", with a link to review it in
  Settings.
- **Red with the triangle** when the app has no content for that ("prioritised, waiting for content").

Important: the goal **can only put first what your child already sees**, their topics and their stage. It never
brings in content outside their world.

With an active goal, in the Studio the recordings under **"What you have recorded"** that serve the goal carry a
**target** next to the word, so at a glance you see which ones already point at what the therapy asked for.

### The mother's document: "The pyramid, at home" — play without screens

Besides the app, there is a **separate document for mom**, for the times when your child is not using screens:
**"The pyramid, at home"**. It opens on her phone (or printed) at the **`/mirada`** page of the app. Your child
never sees it: it is for the adult. **Since Sprint 008 it comes in Spanish and in English** (switch at the top,
written for each language).

It is organised by the **six parts of the pyramid**: pointing, imitation, understanding, joint attention,
communicative intent, and play. **They are not floors**: all of them are worked at once, in the same games. Three
carry the mark **"priority now"** (imitation, joint attention, and play) and come first in the document.

What it holds:

- **What your child already likes**, such as marching, chase games, tickles with their own sound, pointing and
  saying "you", waving hello with hand and voice, vowels. The cards start there on purpose.
- **The pyramid as a map**: one sentence per group, in plain view.
- **56 activity cards** (imitation 12 · joint attention 7 · play 13 · pointing 4 · communicative intent 10 ·
  understanding 10), each with six parts, always in the same order: **Have ready · Do · Your line · Wait and see ·
  It worked if · If nothing happens**. Some say which **step** they suit; that is for finding your place, never a
  target.
- **What not to do**, in ten short rules. The core one: first copy your child, comment instead of asking questions,
  and in the end always give them what they wanted.
- **The traffic light**: if your child looks away, covers their face, or gets upset to get out of the game, you stop
  (in peekaboo, covering the face is the game).

**No log.** An earlier version of the document had a daily log on the phone; it did not turn out to be practical for
mom to keep, so it was removed entirely. The sign that an activity worked is **inside each card** ("It worked
if…"), to watch for while you play, and for some cards over the following days. There is nothing to write down,
send, or save, and the document keeps nothing on mom's phone.

**Where the cards come from:** from the eye-contact capsules of the earlier document and the 50 speech capsules of
the app, each reviewed one by one against fresh research. Some stayed, some were merged into one, some are new, and
those that need the screen or belong to the next stage stayed only in the app. **Nothing changed in the app.**

### Family videos — clips from your gallery, only on this device (new · S8)

The **Studio** has a **"Family videos"** entry. It keeps a few short clips that **show a behaviour** (**asking,
greeting or playing**) so you can watch them with your child, as a prop for the game (co-use: you are right beside
them).

- **How you add one:** **"Choose a video from the gallery"** opens your phone's or computer's file picker. **The app
  never opens the camera**: it does not record, it only keeps a clip you already had. First pick which behaviour
  it shows and, if you like, write **who is in it** (optional, free text, stays here). Write a role, "Grandma", not
  names.
- **Limits:** up to 60 seconds (when the device can read the clip's length) and 50 MB per clip, and up to 12 clips. If one does not fit, the app tells you why
  (not a video, too large, too long, or the shelf is full); if the device has no space it says so and **saves
  nothing half-done**.
- **Where they live:** **only on this device**. They are not uploaded, not sent, not part of any file or copy, and
  the app has no button to download or send them. Changing device means choosing them again.
- **How to delete:** each clip has its own delete button, in **two taps** (the second confirms). And **Settings →
  Delete my data** also deletes every video.
- **Who it is for:** meant for clips of other people your child knows, not for filming your child.
- If the device cannot play a clip, the app says so instead of leaving the screen blank; if a delete fails, it says
  so too.
- **What it does NOT do:** it does not judge, score, compare or say whether your child imitated well: it is only
  the video.

### Stories at home — short stories built on what they enjoy (new · S9)

Short stories of **a few pages**, with **one big word on each page** (single words first). You read them aloud and
your child looks at the word: saying it, copying it or pointing to it all count. **There is no clock and no score.**

- **Where:** your child opens them from **"Today" → "A story to read together"** (`/cuentos`). You set them up in
  **Studio → "Stories at home"**.
- **The app's own:** **six come ready** (four pages each), written in English and in Spanish. They are filled in with
  what you type under **"What your child enjoys"** (general words, like "trains, animals") and **"Words you are
  practicing"** (one word each). Type nothing and they use example words.
- **What travels and what does not:** all a request carries is those **general interests and the practice words**.
  **Never** their name, their age, their voice or anything about a condition; a number is not accepted as an interest,
  and neither is a word this app does not write.
- **New stories, in batches (optional):** **"Download the request"** saves a file. You hand it to Claude Code with
  **/bandeja** (in the project's private folder, which is never uploaded to the repository); it writes a batch of
  stories on the builder's own subscription, **not a pay-per-use service**, and the app **never goes online**.
  Then **"Bring in a batch"** picks that file.
- **You approve each one:** the batch's stories come in **"waiting for you to read"**. Read each all the way through
  and tap **"✓ Approve"** or **"✕ Not this one"**. **Only what you approve** shows up on your child's side. A story
  that talks about deadlines or scores, carries instructions, or uses words this app does not write **does not come
  in**, and the app tells you why.
- **Changing your mind:** under **"Already decided"** you can **withdraw** an approved story (your child stops seeing
  it) or **remove** a rejected one (it frees the spot). If you change what your child enjoys, the recordings of the
  pages that changed stop playing and those pages are read live until you record them again.
- **In your voice:** **"Record my voice"** asks you to read each page; the recording stays **only on this device**,
  like the voice bank. On your child's side **"With my recorded voice"** plays it and **"I'll read it"** leaves just
  the big text. **There is never a machine voice.** Only you record: your child's voice is never saved.
- **Deleting:** **Settings → Delete my data** also deletes the imported stories and the recordings.
- **What it does NOT do:** it does not judge or score, it does not cheer just to cheer, and it sends nothing anywhere.

### Take it to the other home — carry progress to another device (new · S10)

If your child practices in **two homes**, each device keeps its own data and they cannot see each other. This feature
carries what is here to the other home **in a file that you move yourself** (by message, cable or memory card): the
app **does not go online** for this.

- **Where:** **Settings → "Take it to the other home"**.
- **Making the file:** type a **phrase of at least ten characters**, twice, and tap **"Make and download"**. The file
  (`habla-otra-casa-<date>.habla`) is **locked with that phrase**. **The app does not keep it: if it is forgotten, the
  file cannot be opened.**
- **What it carries:** the goal of the week, what you have practiced and what you have achieved. **Your recorded voice
  only if you tick the box.** It **never** carries your child's voice, the videos, the nickname or your settings.
- **Opening one:** in the other home, **"Open a file from the other home"**: choose the file, type the phrase and tap
  **"See what it brings"**. Before anything is written, the app **shows you what will change** (goal before → after,
  games and activities that are added, recordings that come in). Only when you tap **"Yes, add it"** is anything
  written. With the wrong phrase or a file from elsewhere, **nothing comes in**.
- **Nothing is deleted:** what the file brings **is added**; what the device already has stays. If a recording is in a
  format that browser cannot play, the app tells you and **does not bring it in**.
- **What it does NOT do:** it does not sync by itself, it uses no accounts, and it sends nothing anywhere. It is a
  snapshot: do it again whenever you want to catch up.

### Printable sheets — play away from the screen (new · S10)

**Studio → "Printable sheets"** builds **picture cards** and a **turn board** ("My turn / Your turn") to play
sitting together, with no screen.

- **Where they come from:** the words and interests you typed in "Stories at home"; if some are missing, a base set
  fills the gap. **Only the picture and its word are printed**: no name, no age, none of the family's own text.
- **Language:** choose **Spanish or English** for the sheets.
- **Printing:** tap **"Print"** (or use the browser's print menu). You get A4 sheets; the app's controls are not printed.
- **Credits:** **every sheet carries its own footer** with "Sheet n of N" and the **ARASAAC** credit (Sergio Palao,
  Government of Aragón, Creative Commons BY-NC-SA licence, personal and non-commercial use).
- **What it does NOT do:** it scores nothing and measures nothing; it is paper to play with.

### What the app measures and what it does NOT (important)

- **It does measure:** whether there is voice (sound above the noise of the house), how long it is held, and, in
  the rocket, whether the **pitch** of that voice goes up or down, and, in the vowel map (experimental), which
  vowel the shape of the sound **resembles**, always as a "maybe".
- **It does NOT measure:** which word was said, or whether it was said well. No system today assesses reliably the
  pronunciation of a very young child who is just starting to talk, so the app does not pretend to. In "Word and
  picture" the picture lights up with any sound. **That does not mean the word was right**, and the app will never
  say it was.
- **You judge success.** If your child said the word, you know: celebrate with your own words. The app never cheers
  just to cheer (that "great job!" that sounds even when the child said nothing is exactly what we do not want
  here).

### Calm mode

One tap, in the game or in Settings, and the screen drops to dusk colours: **no meter, no goal, and no automatic
celebration**. The balloon does not stop: it **rises while the voice sounds and settles slowly in silence**, like a
real balloon, with no race and no finish line. The rocket does the same: it floats with the voice, with no goal. It
helps when your child is overstimulated or when a game with a goal is too much. It is available in the voice games
(balloon, rocket, word and picture and the vowel map).

There is also a setting to **reduce animation** (and if your system already asks for less motion, the app follows
it without you touching anything). With it on, the word balloons, the milestone confetti, and the rocket's sky
layer are not shown; the text still tells every achievement.

### Light or dark

In Settings, **"Light or dark"** sets how **your** screen looks: it can follow the computer or phone ("System", the
usual choice), or be fixed to light or dark even if the device is set the other way. It is handy at night, beside
your child.

**The child's screen never changes: it is always light.** It does not depend on the time, on the system, or on what
you choose. Their game looks the same every day, and that sameness is part of the design.

## Privacy: what happens to your child's voice

This is the most important promise of the app:

- **Your child's voice is NEVER recorded, saved, or sent off the device.** The sound is analysed on the spot, inside
  the phone or tablet, to work out two numbers, how much energy there is and, in the rocket, how high the pitch is,
  and it is discarded right away (the vowel map also works out the shape of the sound, also on the spot). **Pitch is also data about the voice and follows the same rule:** it is not
  saved, not sent, and not kept anywhere.
- **No servers, no accounts, no cloud.** During play the app makes **no** request to the internet. Automated tests
  check this on every change to the code: if anyone tried to save or send audio, those tests would fail and the
  change would not go in.
- **The camera is never used.**
- **Your family's voice (YOURS) is saved, only here.** If you record your voice in the Studio, those recordings live
  on this device and **never leave it**: they go neither to the internet nor to the project. Automated tests check
  that recording and playing back your voice **triggers no network call at all**. And even with the Studio, **your
  child's voice is never recorded.**
- Switching the app's language sends nothing anywhere.
- All that is kept on the device: the nickname (if you set one), the topics, your settings, which capsules you
  completed, what you marked in "word twins", how many vowel-map spots sounded, if you recorded them **your** voices, and, if you chose them, **the
  family videos** (clips from your own gallery: the app never opens the camera and they never leave here). You can delete it all in
  **Settings → Delete my data**, or only the voice bank with **"Delete only the voice bank"**.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does it help if my child does not talk yet?**
Yes. Many capsules do not need your child to speak: they are things you do (modelling words, waiting in silence,
handing back the full word). And in the balloon, the rocket and word and picture any sound is enough: it moves the balloon, lifts the
rocket, and lights up the picture. It does not have to be a word.

**How much time a day?**
A little and often is better than a lot at once: 5 to 10 minutes is plenty. The app will not ask for more.

**What if my child gets bored or frustrated?**
You stop, and that is all. There is no punishment, no streak to lose, and no defeat screen. If the game feels
heavy, try calm mode, or do the day's activity without the screen.

**Why does the app not tell me whether they said it well?**
Because it would be a lie. Technology that recognises young children's voices makes too many mistakes, and an app
that pretends to know would end up saying "good!" to a child who said nothing. We prefer to tell you only what we
really measured.

**What is "The pyramid, at home" and why is it not inside the app?**
It is mom's document for playing without screens. It lives at the `/mirada` page of the app, but it is a document,
not a game: your child does not use it, the adult does. It is separate on purpose, for the times without screens;
when your child returns to the app, live observation decides which cards move to Today.

**Where did the daily log go?**
It was removed because it did not turn out to be practical for mom to keep. Each card carries its "It worked
if…", which you watch for as you play.

**Does it work without internet?**
Yes, once you have opened it the first time (if you installed it as an app). The games never need the internet.

**Does it replace my child's therapy?**
No, and it never will. It is home practice to go along with what your team, whether speech and language, occupational
therapy, psychology, or teaching, is already doing.

**Can my child play alone?**
No, by design. Each activity is built for an adult and a child together, and your line always comes before the game.

**Why do I record MY voice and not my child's?**
Because the voice that helps a child learn a word is that of someone who loves them: yours. And because your
child's voice is delicate data that this app promised to **never** keep. Your voice, on the other hand, is under
your control: you record it, play it, switch it off, or delete it whenever you like.

**Does the voice I record go anywhere?**
No. It lives only on this device. If you lend the tablet to someone else or change device, those recordings do not
travel with you; you record them again where they will be used.

**Can I use the app in English?**
Yes. Choose it in Settings → "Idioma / Language". The word and twin games, the weekly Goal and mom's documents are
practised and read in English, with content that belongs to that language.

**Does the app record or film my child for the videos?**
No. It never opens the camera. The videos are clips you already had in your gallery and choose yourself; they are
saved only on this device and you delete them whenever you like.
